Culinary arts · Community leadership · Hawaiʻi

225 hours of growth

My internship at Kāle’s Hale was an opportunity to grow as a cook, a baker and a professional. By October 4, 2026, I had completed my required 225 hours, but my experience there was only the beginning of another chapter.

225Required hours completed
2026Culinary internship
Week 1 · Orientation and first service

Finding my place in the kitchen

My first week at Kāle’s Hale began with a Thursday orientation and kitchen tour. I started by washing dishes, which helped me learn where equipment, dishes and supplies were stored. On Friday, I ran the line during my first rush. I was proud of being able to keep up and work under pressure. On Sunday, I prepared crab salad, washed dishes and helped prepare and wrap over 100 laulau.

Making laulau for the first time in my life was especially meaningful. I had the opportunity to connect with traditional Hawaiian food while developing practical culinary skills. Throughout the week, I strengthened my communication, teamwork, organization and time management, and saw how classroom lessons translate into a professional kitchen. My goal moving forward was to become more confident and independent on the line, improve my speed and organization, and continue asking questions and learning new techniques.

Week 2 · September 6

Building confidence in meat preparation

I assisted with food preparation, worked the line, washed dishes and maintained my station. I began learning how to properly cut different meats and prepare sauces and soups. Meat cutting was a skill I had limited experience with, and I learned how consistent cuts affect cooking and presentation. I became more familiar with the equipment and daily routine, and developed my communication, teamwork, organization and time-management skills.

Week 3 · September 13

Laulau, knife skills and kitchen organization

I continued making laulau and developing my meat-cutting skills, paying attention to the size and consistency of each cut. I also helped organize the walk-in refrigerator and clean the kitchen for service. These tasks taught me that working in a kitchen involves much more than cooking: proper storage, sanitation, organization and teamwork are essential. I wanted to become faster, more consistent and more independent without always needing to be told what to do.

Week 4 · September 18

Leading by example

I continued assisting with preparation, cleaning and daily kitchen operations. One of the biggest challenges I observed was maintaining the cleanliness of the back of the house. Culinary school has taught me to clean as I go, wash dishes after using them, take out trash before it builds up, and properly label, date and store food. I focused on my own work habits and tried to set a positive example. I wanted to leave a clean station and make the kitchen easier for everyone to work in.

Week 5 · September 2026

Company party and a new baking opportunity

Before the company party, I cleaned and organized the walk-in refrigerator. Later that evening, I helped cook for the party, and Chef Kale taught me five different ways to make a seafood boil. It was a completely new experience for me and an enjoyable opportunity to learn while cooking for a larger group.

One of the most meaningful developments was that Chef Kale recognized how much I love baking. He purchased an oven for Kāle’s Hale and invited me to develop baked goods such as scones, pies and pastries. He asked me to prepare a wishlist of equipment and supplies. Being trusted with this opportunity gave me the chance to apply my baking and pastry education in a restaurant environment and think about efficient, consistent production.

Final journal · October 4, 2026

Experimenting with ʻulu flour

As I approached the end of my required hours, I spent more time experimenting with baking and ʻulu flour. I genuinely enjoy baking, and exploring the possibilities of a local ingredient was especially exciting. Chef Kale gave me the space to be creative and develop my own ideas. I learned that experimentation and mistakes are part of becoming a better cook and baker.

I thoroughly enjoyed working under both Chef Kale and Chef Aaron. Every day brought a new opportunity to learn about cooking, baking, kitchen management and the industry. Their environment made me feel comfortable asking questions, practicing new techniques and continuing to grow.

Completing my hours — and looking forward

When I completed my required 225 hours, I decided that I wanted to continue baking at Kāle’s Hale on weekends. I also looked forward to opportunities to participate in night markets, sell products and keep a portion of the profits. These opportunities would allow me to practice entrepreneurship, product development, pricing and customer interaction alongside my culinary skills.

My internship taught me that culinary arts is about teamwork, cleanliness, organization, creativity, culture, consistency and being willing to learn every day. Completing my hours felt less like an ending and more like the beginning of another chapter in my culinary career.
