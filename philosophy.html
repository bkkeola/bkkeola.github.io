Culinary arts · Community leadership · Hawaiʻi

Work with aloha. Lead with purpose.

<

As a Native Hawaiian culinary student, I believe the way I work and treat people is just as important as the food I prepare. These values guide my decisions in the kitchen, at school and in the community.

Kūlia i ka Nuʻu · Personal excellence

Strive to reach the summit

I challenge myself to go beyond completing an assigned task. During my internship at Kāle’s Hale, I progressed from learning garde manger and food preparation to independently opening the restaurant and beginning bento production. I strive to improve my technique, consistency and time management while remaining receptive to feedback.

ʻIke Loa · Learning

Seek knowledge and wisdom

I approach each shift as an opportunity to learn. Chef Kāle demonstrated different approaches to preparing a seafood boil during a company gathering, reminding me that education can occur outside the classroom. I ask questions, observe experienced chefs and apply what I learn to unfamiliar tasks.

Lōkahi · Teamwork

Work together in unity

At Kāle’s Hale, preparing laulau, producing bentos, washing dishes and organizing the walk-in required cooperation. I help wherever I am needed rather than limiting myself to my assigned station. At KCC, I also coordinate volunteers and community partners, recognizing that shared goals require respectful communication and collaboration.

Alakaʻi · Leadership

Guide others through trust

Chef Kāle’s willingness to recognize potential in his employees taught me that leadership is about helping others grow. I apply that lesson in my work with the Service & Sustainability Learning Program, including organizing the Alani Children’s Center gingerbread house event. I strive to lead by example, communicate clearly and follow through on my responsibilities.

Mālama · Stewardship

Care for people and resources

At Kāle’s Hale, I observed how luʻau leaves and meat trimmings could be used thoughtfully to reduce waste. I practice stewardship by respecting ingredients, maintaining clean work areas and using resources responsibly. My community service work also reinforces my responsibility to care for the people and places that sustain us.

Pono · Rightness and balance

Act with integrity

When Chef Kāle trusted me to open the restaurant independently, I became responsible for maintaining standards even when he was not present. I practice pono by cleaning as I work, labeling and storing food properly, communicating honestly and taking ownership of my mistakes. I want my coworkers to know they can depend on me.

My commitment

These values remind me that success in hospitality is not measured solely by the quality of a dish or the profitability of a business. It is also reflected in the way we support others, honor our culture and care for our community. I hope to carry these principles into my future nonprofit bakery by creating a supportive workplace where people have meaningful opportunities to learn and succeed.

Values and definitions adapted from Kapiʻolani Community College’s HOST program, Week 6 Hawaiian Values course material.
