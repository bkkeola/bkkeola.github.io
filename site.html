Kāle’s Hale

Hawaiian fusion · All-you-can-eat yakiniku · Kalihi, Honolulu

About the restaurant

Where cultures come together

Kāle’s Hale, located at 1339 N. School Street in Honolulu, combines Hawaiian culinary heritage with Japanese and Chinese influences in an interactive Korean-style tabletop grilling experience. Its official website describes the restaurant as a pioneer of Hawaiian fusion cuisine, with a focus on ancestral ingredients such as kalo, ʻulu, ʻōpihi and limu.

Chef Kale’s Hawaiian, Japanese and Chinese heritage inspires the menu. His culinary career evolved from sushi cakes and food trucks to creating a Hawaiian fusion restaurant concept in Kalihi.

Signature cuisine

From land to table

Guests can enjoy premium Kagoshima A5 wagyu, locally sourced ingredients and the restaurant’s signature luau soup. Lunch sets and bento boxes offer another way to experience the menu, with local-style sides, rice and sauces.

Innovation

Bentoyaki

The restaurant developed Bentoyaki, an all-in-one disposable tabletop yakiniku set designed to bring the grilling experience to outings, picnics and other gatherings.

Recognition

2026 ʻIlima Award

The official website identifies Kāle’s Hale as the 2026 ʻIlima Award winner for Best All-You-Can-Eat.

Mission and values

Rather than attributing an unpublished formal mission statement to the restaurant, I understand its purpose through its public emphasis on Hawaiian culture, local pride, culinary creativity and bringing people together through food. Its approach connects ancestral ingredients and traditions with contemporary dining experiences.

My role as a culinary intern

During my 225-hour internship, I worked on the line, prepared crab salad, helped wrap over 100 laulau, developed my meat-cutting skills, prepared sauces and soups, and assisted with sanitation and walk-in organization. I also had opportunities to learn from Chef Kale and Chef Aaron, prepare food for a company gathering, and explore baking with ʻulu flour. These experiences allowed me to connect what I learned in culinary school with the restaurant’s daily operations.
