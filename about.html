My beginning · My purpose

Where it all began

My culinary journey began in a place that many people would not expect: the Women’s Community Correctional Center (WCCC). During my incarceration, I enrolled in the culinary program taught by Chef Lee Shinsato. At a time when I struggled to find where I belonged, the kitchen became a place where I discovered confidence, creativity and purpose.

Chef Lee’s patience, guidance and passion for teaching changed the direction of my life. He saw potential in me and encouraged me to pursue an education beyond those walls. He taught me more than how to cook; he taught me discipline, perseverance and pride in my work. He is my inspiration and a major reason I have made it this far.

A full-circle moment

After completing the culinary program at WCCC, I continued my education at Kapiʻolani Community College. One of the most meaningful opportunities has been returning the support I received by volunteering alongside Chef Lee at My Sister’s Keeper, a culinary fundraiser supporting women and children, including women transitioning from incarceration. Through that experience, I also connected with Chef Kale, whose mentorship at Kāle’s Hale helped me grow as a culinary professional.

My past is part of my story, but it does not define the limits of my future. I am proud of the education I have earned, the people I have met and the community I have become part of.

My future: Rooted & Rising Bakehouse

I aspire to establish a Hawaiʻi-inspired nonprofit bakery that provides training, mentorship and meaningful employment opportunities primarily for justice-involved women and at-risk youth. I want to create the kind of environment that Chef Lee created for me: one where people can discover their abilities, build confidence and see a future for themselves.

I am pursuing my culinary education with that purpose in mind. Every class, service project and internship experience is another step toward using food to build opportunities for others.
